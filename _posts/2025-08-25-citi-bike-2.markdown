This project was my **first hands-on experience with SQL and Power BI**, where I explored real-world bike-sharing data to practice analysis, data cleaning, and visualization.

---

## 🎯 Project Goal
Learn and apply **SQL** for querying, cleaning, and exploring data.<br/>
Build an **interactive Power BI dashboard** to visualize trends and insights.<br/>
Practice **data storytelling** using a public dataset.

---

## 📊 Dataset
The dataset comes from Kaggle: [NYC Citi Bike Sampled Data (2013–2017)](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/fatihb/citibike-sampled-data-2013-2017).  

For this project, I focused on the **January–March 2017 (Q1)** subset.<br/>  
**The data includes:**
- Trip start/end times and stations
- Trip duration 
- Rider demographics (age, gender, user type)  

---

## 🛠 Tools & Skills Practiced
- **SQL (MySQL Workbench)** – Joins, aggregations, filtering, anomaly detection.
- **Power BI** – Data modeling, calculated measures (DAX), and dashboard creation.
- **Data storytelling** – Translating findings into meaningful business insights.

---

## 🔍 Key Steps
1. **Data Cleaning & Preparation**
   - Removed anomalies (e.g., invalid birth years).
   - Parsed and transformed date/time columns.
   - Identified peak/trough outliers and validated with domain context.

2. **SQL Analysis**
   - Queries for **daily and hourly ridership trends**.
   - Calculations of **average trip duration**, **repeat riders**, and **user type breakdown**.

3. **Power BI Dashboard**
   - Built interactive visuals:
     - Line chart: trips over time
     - Heatmap: weekday vs. hour trip patterns
     - Demographics breakdown
     - Map: most popular stations
   - KPI cards summarizing key metrics.

---

## 📈 Insights Gained
- **Weekday commuter trends:** Strong peaks at 8–9 AM and 5–7 PM.
- **Subscriber dominance:** Majority of trips are by subscribers; casual users spike on weekends.
- **Station hotspots:** Central Manhattan consistently drives the highest trip volume.

---

## 💡 Learnings
- First experience **combining SQL for backend prep with Power BI for front-end visualization**.
- Gained practice in **data cleaning** and understanding why anomalies matter.
- Improved skills in **presenting insights visually for non-technical audiences**.
